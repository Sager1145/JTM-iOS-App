# Canada rail package — sources and method

`ca-2025.json` is built by the same fail-closed pipeline as the United States
package. Its 66 published lines use 38 route-isolated government/operator GIS
geometries, 27 official GTFS shapes that pass an independent track
cross-check, and one NARN routing. It contains no station-to-station fallback
chords. Two lines, TTC Line 1 and Line 2, are drawn from audited
OpenStreetMap relations under the evidence rule described below.

Canadian reviewed spatial sources include City of Edmonton LRT tracks,
TransLink's official system GIS, Toronto/TTC subway and streetcar track,
Ottawa Trillium alignments, Ontario's ORWN and federal NRWN track networks,
and Québec MTQ railway GIS where an operational feature explicitly identifies
the passenger service.

GO Transit publishes seven ORWN-routed geometries: Barrie, Lakeshore East,
Lakeshore West and its Hamilton branch, Milton, Richmond Hill, and Stouffville.
Kitchener and UP Express use route-filtered federal NRWN geometry. GTFS
supplies station identity, order, service, and colour.

All five exo commuter routes use reproducibly normalized Québec MTQ geometry.
TTC 506 Carlton uses City of Toronto track geometry. TTC Line 1 and Line 2
are drawn from audited OpenStreetMap route relations (102388 and 102386,
ODbL, © OpenStreetMap contributors) accepted on recorded evidence: the City's
subway route layer is byte-for-byte the TTC GTFS shape and is a schematic
route line, and where the City's own topographic subway-track survey can see
the railway it prefers the relation (`validate-ttc-subway-osm.py`, whose
numbers the registry's `osmRelationEvidenceByRouteId` quotes). No published
survey covers the tunnels; the package records the unmatched vertex count.
The streetcar network carries its daytime identities (501, 504, 505, 506, 510,
512 in TTC red) with the 3xx Blue Night routes folded into them, and trips
the TTC publishes as replacement buses are excluded from pattern selection.
Routes 503 and 507 remain blocked because every current trip is a replacement
bus. STM is blocked because its published shapefile is documented as
converted from GTFS shapes/stops, and REM remains blocked without an
independently surveyed centreline.

Cross-border station ownership is country-specific: Niagara Falls `NFL` stays
on the US side, `NFS` stays on the Canadian side, and Saint-Lambert has one
Canadian identity. No coordinate is inferred merely from a station name.

Names, time zones, route colours, and station order come from each operator's
publication. See [`us-2025.sources.md`](./us-2025.sources.md) for the shared
authority order and release rules, and
[`na-2025-line-review.md`](./na-2025-line-review.md) for every published line
and blocked diagnostic row.
