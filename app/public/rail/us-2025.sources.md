# United States rail package — sources and method

`us-2025.json` is a fail-closed `compact-v1` package. Its 348 published lines
use 261 route-isolated government/operator GIS geometries, 53 official GTFS
shapes that pass an independent track cross-check, and 34 NARN routings. It
contains no station-to-station fallback chords.

## Reviewed station approach overrides (2026-09-08)

Four services (three primarily NARN-routed and the GTFS-derived Pennsylvanian)
now carry explicit secondary-source `stationApproachRepairs` metadata. Acela and Silver Meteor previously bypassed
Philadelphia's lower-level through platforms on the west, with 336–406 m
station-anchor chords. Crescent and Silver Meteor reached Washington's
northern terminal approach after coming through First Street Tunnel, then
retraced northbound. These were source-routing/anchor defects, not curves to
smooth in either renderer.

Pennsylvanian also approached and left Philadelphia on a northwest/southeast
diagonal across the yard: its GTFS-derived intervals contained repeated
collinear 530–580 m edges instead of the curved platform approach. Both legs
now use Keystone Service's reviewed FRA/BTS NTAD geometry, joining at exact
existing vertices west of Ardmore and northeast of North Philadelphia. The
catalog preserves the contiguous donor interval/vertex ranges across those
station boundaries. Pennsylvanian's real reversal at Philadelphia remains:
both legs use the north approach to the lower-level platforms. Its route-wide
`gtfs-shape` classification remains; the replacement windows explicitly name
`amtrak-ntad-keystone-service` and its government provenance.

The replacement windows preserve contiguous, exact coordinates already in the
published package; every splice lands on an identical existing vertex. No
coordinate was interpolated, averaged, offset, or joined across a gap. The
Philadelphia windows and Crescent's Washington window come from Northeast
Regional's reviewed FRA/BTS NTAD alignment (`amtrak-ntad-northeast-regional`),
whose publisher URL, raw SHA-256 and normalized SHA-256 remain in
`verifiedOfficialNetworks` and are copied into the repair catalog. This is
US federal public-domain, WGS84 geometry.

Silver Meteor's Washington window uses the accepted Amtrak Palmetto GTFS
alignment, with exact shared vertices about 0.8 km south and 1.7 km northeast
of the previous anchor. Its retained vertices are independently within 9.27 m
of the reviewed Northeast Regional NTAD corridor. The original Amtrak feed
archive/hash was unavailable in this checkout, so its provenance explicitly
identifies the retained package and donor-line SHA-256, the operator's
[GTFS publication](https://content.amtrak.com/content/gtfs/GTFS.zip), and
operator GTFS terms; it does not assert a newly downloaded or verified feed.

Amtrak's [regional connectivity study, chapter 2](https://www.amtrak.com/content/dam/projects/dotcom/english/public/documents/ny-penn-capacity-expansion-feasibility-study/penn-capacity-expansion-feasibility-study-chapter-2-planning-regional-connectivity.pdf)
places Amtrak/NJ Transit on Philadelphia's lower-level platforms, separate
from SEPTA's upper level. Amtrak's [August 24, 2026 infrastructure notice](https://media.amtrak.com/2026/08/amtrak-announces-pause-for-progress-infrastructure-upgrade-in-washington-d-c-for-nine-days-this-october/)
confirms First Street Tunnel serves all Amtrak/VRE movements between
Washington Union Station and Virginia, including Crescent and Silver Meteor.
These publications establish the service topology; the retained surveyed
and operator coordinates establish the geometry.

`app/scripts/railway/na-station-approach-repairs.json` archives the exact rejected
and replacement windows, source evidence, donor interval/vertex ranges, and
input checksums. The raw NARN and normalized official downloads were not
available under this checkout, the sibling builder checkout, Documents, or
`/tmp`; the reproducible owning layer for this release is therefore a reviewed
post-build override:

```bash
python3 app/scripts/railway/repair-na-station-approaches.py --app-root app
python3 -m unittest discover -s app/scripts/railway/tests -p test_na_station_approaches.py -v
```

Run the override after rebuilding the US package and before regenerating
display data, audit ledgers and cross-platform fixtures. It refuses changed
source windows for renewed review and is a no-op on repaired output. It
updates only ten interval rows, five station memberships, their solver
sections and four route lengths; names, IDs and station readings remain
unchanged. Route-wide sources describe the retained primary geometry;
window metadata identifies the secondary source boundaries. Catalog extensions
refresh every retained repair record's catalog digest without reapplying its
geometry, and the package ledger retains all five repairs.
Affected full-line comparison records are explicitly marked as preceding this
override. Their old survey figures are not claimed as measurements of the
replacement windows or as a fresh full-line audit.

## Authority order

1. A reviewed operator or government railway centreline, stored as a
   route-isolated GeoJSON with exact publisher, URL, raw SHA-256, and normalized
   SHA-256 provenance.
2. The operator's GTFS for route/station identity, station order, service
   pattern, and published colour. GTFS geometry is accepted only after the
   independent FRA/OSM comparison and final geometry gates.
3. FRA/BTS North American Rail Network routing for mainline services, using
   the operator publication as the service corridor and station-order source.
4. OpenStreetMap is a visual/track cross-check matching the WebUI basemap. It
   is not used to override a conflicting reviewed official GIS centreline, and
   no final US line is sourced directly from OSM.

The reviewed official source families include MTA subway and LIRR, CTA,
NORTA, LA Metro, SFMTA, Sound Transit, VTA, MassGIS/MBTA, SEPTA,
MARTA/Atlanta, Miami-Dade, Maryland iMAP/MTA, WMATA/DCGIS, Pittsburgh PRT,
Houston METRO, DART/NCTCOG, Detroit People Mover, CAGIS Cincinnati, Utah UGRC,
Metra/City of Chicago/RTA, Metropolitan Council, NJ Transit/PATH,
Metrolink/SCAG, FDOT Rail LRS, FRA/BTS Amtrak route GIS, and Oregon Metro RLIS
for Portland Streetcar route 194. Exact endpoints and hashes are in the build
manifest and package
`verifiedOfficialNetworks` metadata.

`a96c8634ce69393626751f027dd444a120b9052d221a27fa2e982a4b81fda120`).

cross-check confirms on active rail. Metra Electric remains on the City of
Chicago KML whose raw SHA-256 is unchanged; all four decoded parts have zero
unmatched vertices and maxima of 19.01 m, 12.59 m, 9.40 m and 12.52 m against

## Operator-shape-only geometry, accepted by name (2026-09-02)

A new `geometrySource: "gtfs-official"` class covers routes the fail-closed
"independent survey unavailable" gate would otherwise withhold forever, not
because a source disagrees with the operator's GTFS but because no
second, independent source exists at all. The registry's per-route
`acceptOperatorShapeByRouteId` key names each such route explicitly and
carries the evidence for why no survey exists; every other geometry gate
(station snapping, chord/detour rejection, internal-reversal and
display-blocked-interval checks) still runs unchanged, and a route absent
from the key gets no benefit from it. It currently covers Detroit QLINE
route 13578 (M-1 Rail, the streetcar's non-profit operator, publishes no
open GIS survey centreline for the Woodward Ave loop) and SEPTA routes G1
(Route 15) and T2 (Route 11), whose one candidate independent source — SEPTA
Planning Division's own trolley GIS layer — routes every other SEPTA
trolley route (D1, D2, T1, T3, T4, T5) cleanly and now supplies those six
routes' `officialNetworkByRouteId` geometry directly, but leaves a real gap
across G1's Richmond St reverse branch and does not reach T2's Woodland
Av/Island Av terminal. QLINE's own GTFS `shapes.txt` is additionally flagged
schematic (a straight-line reconstruction of its own stop list), so it does
not build even with the survey requirement waived; Detroit's Open Data
Portal separately publishes an official `QLine_Route` layer that fits all 12
QLINE stops within 13 m, which a future change could wire in as a verified
source instead. SEPTA B2's Fern Rock terminal (stop 841) was a station-identity
error, not a missing survey: stop 841 (served only by bus routes 28/57/70)
and stop 20965 (served by B1/B2/B3) publish the identical name "Fern Rock
Transit Center" 21 m apart, so SEPTA's `stationIdentityGroups` now lists the
rail platform first, and B2 ships on its own operator GTFS shape.

## Fail-closed rules

- Missing or invalid official-network provenance blocks that route; no GTFS or
  NARN fallback is allowed for a route configured to require the official GIS.
- A failed trunk suppresses every generated branch.
- Collinear densification does not hide an endpoint chord.
- Internal near-reversals, broken seams, station-order jumps, repeated
  non-loop station calls, and unresolved station anchors are release blockers.
- A government layer documented as converted from GTFS cannot independently
  verify the same GTFS.
- A provenance-verified operator/government centreline remains visible when a
  lower-authority visual cross-check disagrees. The exact intervals and
  deviation remain audit warnings; only GTFS/NARN candidates without that
  primary-source authority are withheld from display.
- Colours must be the exact GTFS `route_color` or an exact registered official
  route palette. Random/default/generated/fallback colours are rejected.

The WebUI and iOS renderers retain round line caps and joins; build-time
grooming also preserves curved track instead of replacing it with schematic
station connectors.

See [`na-2025.acceptance.md`](./na-2025.acceptance.md),
[`us-2025.audit.json`](./us-2025.audit.json), and
[`na-2025-line-review.md`](./na-2025-line-review.md) for current counts,
findings, blocked diagnostic rows, and artifact hashes.

## Hoboken approach repair — 2026-09-06

PATH route 859 uses NJ Transit GIS feature 10 (`Hoboken-33rd St`), whose
two MultiLineString endpoints at [-74.0340874586921, 40.7324962608071]
and [-74.0340876519808, 40.7324962896805] differ by 0.0166 m. The PATH feed
now opts into the existing degree-one endpoint joining rule with a 0.1 m
limit. This reconnects the authority's own Hoboken approach; no surveyed
vertices are relocated and no new survey source is introduced. Coordinates
remain WGS84. The source and existing attribution/licence terms are unchanged.

Source: [NJ Transit GIS PATH layer](https://services6.arcgis.com/M0t0HPE53pFK525U/arcgis/rest/services/PATH/FeatureServer/1).
The local normalized source retains raw SHA-256
`9382638edebbbf3c2345d0804088667ed6d5aafcea2bd83f058e17ee540ec724`
and the original feature's 2021-06-21 edit timestamp. Reviewed on 2026-09-06
against local PATH GTFS (`517.zip`, parent 26730) and the existing independent
OSM reference. The rebuilt line records 76 matched comparison vertices,
zero unmatched, and a maximum deviation of 22.67 m (25 m metro limit).

The Christopher Street–Hoboken interval restores 1.105 km of omitted track;
the line is 5.737 km, previously 4.632 km. All three PATH Hoboken anchors
are [-74.029208, 40.735451], approximately 45.5 m from the published GTFS
station. They share `us-official-hoboken` with NJT. Station grouping uses
published station coordinates independently of display anchors; the 140/400 m
grouping thresholds are unchanged. Only the three Hoboken-serving PATH lines
were merged from the joint NJT/PATH candidate, with per-line build provenance.

## Service-pattern display repairs — 2026-09-09

The builder draws a line's station list as the union of every trip pattern
in the operator's GTFS, so atypical, diversion, never-operating ("canonical")
and late-night-only patterns had added stations and lanes that the line's
regular service does not serve. Thirteen lines were corrected in place with
`app/scripts/railway/repair-na-service-patterns.py` and the evidence catalog
`app/scripts/railway/na-service-pattern-repairs.json`; the package records the
change list, input checksums and catalog SHA-256 under `servicePatternRepair`.
The same corrections are expressed for the next rebuild as registry knobs in
`na-feeds.json` (`excludeTripsByRouteId`, `excludeStopIdsByRouteId`,
`loopLeadInRouteIds`, Miami `stationIdentityNearMeters`) and as the
never-operating-service rule in `lib/na_gtfs.py`.

- MBTA Green Line B and C now end at Government Center (23 and 20 stations);
  the Medford/Tufts patterns are MBTA route-pattern typicality 3 (atypical).
- Providence/Stoughton: the "via Fairmount" diversion branch (`-b5`) is gone,
  and Forest Hills and South Attleboro carry no Providence dot — both appear
  only in canonical (never-operating) and typicality-3 patterns.
- NYC Subway 2, 3, 4, <7>, A, D and Q lost the local stations they pass in
  regular daytime express service (late-night-only calls), per the MTA line
  maps: 12, 3, 5, 5, 22, 6 and 5 stations respectively.
- Metromover Inner Loop is a closed 8-station `isLoop` (the seam was two GTFS
  stop_ids at one coordinate, drawn as a ninth station).
- Portland Streetcar A Loop is a closed 28-station `isLoop`; NW 13th & Lovejoy
  (an NS Line stop) is gone and the closing interval NW 10th & Johnson →
  NW 9th & Lovejoy follows the two street blocks of the loop's own centreline.

Each catalog entry cites its operator source (MBTA V3 API route_patterns,
mbta.com timetables, MTA line maps, Miami-Dade Metromover map, TriMet GTFS)
and the display rule it enforces. Station-to-station chords were not invented;
geometry outside the Portland closing interval is unchanged.

### Second batch — station labels and intersection stop pairs (2026-09-09)

A per-line ledger over every shipped US and Canadian line (436 lines, generic
rules plus the four analysis datasets) found two further display-rule
violations, applied through the same cumulative catalog (`renameStation`,
`mergeStations` ops; entries carry `id`s and the package records which ids
are folded in):

- One station, one label. Acela, Northeast Regional and Lake Shore Limited
  carried two stations both labelled "Boston" (South Station and Back Bay,
  1.75 km apart; the MBTA rows of the same station ids already say South
  Station / Back Bay). Seven Connecticut lines carried two "New Haven" rows
  (Union Station and State Street, 0.93 km apart); the State Street row is now
  "New Haven State Street". The L carried two "14 St" rows (6 Av and 8 Av,
  0.56 km apart), now "6 Av" and "8 Av" as the MTA names them.
- One intersection, one stop slot. Consecutive stops ≤ 26 m apart that are the
  same corner or the same station's platforms were merged into one station
  (McKinney Avenue Trolley ×2, Powell–Hyde and Powell–Mason cable cars, SEPTA
  Route 15 ×2, TTC 505 Dundas West outer platform); intervals were joined, no
  geometry was altered. Directional stop pairs further apart were left as
  published.

### Third batch — verified against the operators' current GTFS (2026-09-09)

The four affected feeds (MBTA, MTA New York City Transit, Miami-Dade Transit,
TriMet/Portland Streetcar) were downloaded into the gitignored
`app/data/raw/na-rail/` together with their official network GIS, and a scoped
rebuild was compared line by line with the shipped package. Fifty-seven of the
sixty-five lines of those feeds are identical (station sets and loop flags);
the registry knobs now reproduce every in-place repair. The comparison also
caught two errors in the earlier catalog and one builder defect:

- D: 155 St is a regular stop (82 % of weekday daytime D trips call there;
  only peak-direction rush expresses skip it). The row removed by the first
  batch was restored (`insertStation`), splitting the merged 145 St–161 St
  interval at that station's own anchor vertex. The registry exclusion for
  the D now lists only the late-night-only 4 Av and Central Park West locals.
- MTA registry stop ids for routes 4, <7> and A were corrected by name against
  stops.txt; the <7> list keeps only the five local stations current <7>
  trips call at under the Flushing Line construction pattern.
- F / FX: the station "Avenue N" was shipped as "Avenue" because the builder's
  directional-suffix stripper removed the letter; renamed in the package and
  guarded in the builder.
- Portland Streetcar A Loop: the builder dropped the closed loop because the
  official-network routing picked two different snap points for the seam
  station; `route_stations` now reconciles the seam the way it reconciles
  every interior station.

After the seam fix, the scoped TriMet rebuild produced the A Loop as a closed
28-station line routed entirely on the Oregon Metro RLIS official network, and
that geometry was spliced into the shipped package with
`splice-na-line-geometry.py` (248 → 224 vertices, 9.931 → 9.745 km),
replacing the two-block closing interval the first batch had synthesised.

- N / R / W: the Manhattan BMT station us-official-59-st carried the complex
  label "59 St", which on the N and R collided with the Brooklyn 4 Av "59 St";
  the Manhattan rows are now "Lexington Av/59 St" (MTA parent R11). Lines whose
  own station is named "59 St" (4/5/6) are unchanged. The builder gained a
  per-line rule that restores each station's own published name when two
  different stations of one line would otherwise share a label.
