## Osaka Loop direction (strongest actionable finding)

All four records are stored north/origin → Tennoji/south. Their western-loop corridor corresponds to **内回り**, geographically **counterclockwise** when oriented this way. Return operation is the inverse. This is a geographic description, not an assertion about forward/reverse storage order; integration must inspect the schema semantics.

- Rapid service direct proof: JR West incident release dated 2026-05-19, https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/2026/05/19/items/260519_00_press_kyoubashi.pdf , p.1, explicitly names an inner-loop Kansai Airport/Kishuji Rapid originating at Kyobashi, via Osaka, for Kansai Airport/Wakayama. Supports current Kyobashi outer origin and southbound inner-loop direction.
- Western-loop passage/older direction proof: JR West 2015 revision attachment published 2014-12-19, https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/items/141219_00_honsya.pdf , section IV/p.7: increases Osaka-origin through Kansai Airport/Kishuji Rapid services into Hanwa, and explicitly labels Osaka departures toward Nishikujo, Tennoji, Kansai Airport and Wakayama as inner-loop. Supports these services' western corridor by 2015, not their 1994/1999 opening footprints.
- Unambiguous station order/direction proof: https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/items/221129_00_press_omisoka.pdf , p.3 labels Tennoji→Nishikujo→Osaka→Kyobashi outer-loop and Tennoji→Kyobashi→Osaka→Nishikujo inner-loop. This is a direction definition, not rapid/express-specific historical evidence.
- Kuroshio direct western passage: https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/2017/11/page_11459.html , dated 2017-11-10, identifies Kuroshio 23 from Kyoto to Shirahama passing its scheduled Nishikujo stop; affected inner-loop local passengers continued from Nishikujo to Tennoji. Also explicitly identifies Kuroshio 25 Shin-Osaka→Shingu. This directly supports western passage and those dated endpoints.
- Haruka/Kuroshio shared western route: https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/items/221209_00_press_senrokirikae.pdf , p.1, says the Shin-Osaka→Nishikujo track normally carries both express services and freight, and suspends Haruka throughout and Kuroshio Kyoto/Shin-Osaka→Tennoji on 2023-02-12. Together with the explicit western-loop direction definition this supports selecting the western arc for the catalog's Osaka→Tennoji display proxy. It does **not** prove the proxy reproduces the Umeda freight line or Osaka mainline station passenger stops before 2023.

Recommended JSON: explicitly encode counterclockwise (or equivalent inner-loop schema value) on the Osaka Loop legs of `kansai-airport-rapid`, `kishuji-rapid`, `haruka`, `kuroshio`, with the specific official sources above. Do not derive the enum from stored station order alone. Retain freight-geometry limitations and never treat this display selection as a physical track connection.

## Haruka

- Exact service onset: 1994-09-04. JR West official chronology https://www.westjr.co.jp/company/info/history/ , 1994 entries, distinguishes airport line opening 1994-06-15 from Haruka debut 1994-09-04. Anniversary release https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/items/240802_00_press_Haruka30theventcampaign.pdf , p.1 repeats exact Haruka onset. **These text excerpts do not establish initial Kyoto endpoint.** The anniversary p.3 describes merchandise reproducing an opening-era route map, but screenshot tooling returned no inspectable pixels; do not claim its map was read.
- Official 2019 new-car release https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/2019/06/page_14402.html explicitly gives operating section Maibara/Kyoto→Kansai Airport, so confirms those outer endpoints in that dated snapshot.
- JR West data books search-index extracts show Maibara extension in **2003-06**: https://www.westjr.co.jp/company/info/issue/data/pdf/data2019.pdf . Reader rejects full PDF (30,080,706 bytes). Treat month as source-discovery information only; exact 2003-06-01 opening remains unverified here.
- https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/items/201218_00_keihanshin.pdf , pp.2,7: explicit Yasu-origin/terminating Haruka workings; notes partial regular-train suspensions since 2020-04-24. It does not, in extracted text, isolate Maibara services as suspended from that date. Preserve uncertainty rather than encoding an unproven all-Maibara suspension boundary.
- https://www.westjr.co.jp/press/article/items/221209_00_press_senrokirikae.pdf confirms total Haruka suspension on **2023-02-12 only**; Kuroshio north of Tennoji likewise suspended that day. This is sufficiently exact for a one-day applicability hole if the model supports it.

Recommended: service onset metadata `1994-09-04` is supported, but do not make the current Maibara/Yasu full footprint valid at opening. Retain unresolved pre-2005 endpoint review, or implement earlier variants only after evidence for their actual outer terminals. Correct current note that asserts all Maibara workings stopped 2020-04-24 unless separately sourced. The existing 2021 split source is relevant but alone does not certify complete daily service.

## Kansai Airport Rapid and Kishuji Rapid

- JR West data book search-index extracts https://www.westjr.co.jp/company/info/issue/data/pdf/data2025.pdf state Kansai Airport Rapid began **1994-09** and Kishuji Rapid began **1999-05** under Osaka Loop chronology. Full PDF reader rejects 32,321,173-byte file. This is official indexed evidence at month precision, not a directly reviewed exact-day timetable.
- Exact days 1994-09-04 and 1999-05-10 are consistently discoverable in secondary sites but were not found in directly readable primary evidence during this bounded research. No recommendation to promote those exact dates solely from secondary repetition.
- Strong current endpoints/direction proof is 2026-05-19 incident PDF above: Kyobashi→Osaka→Kansai Airport/Wakayama. The dated 2014-12-19 release confirms the west side by 2015. Neither proves opening outer endpoints.

Recommended: repair direction now; add only month-precision historical evidence notes/status if schema accepts uncertainty. Do not silently use the first day or last day of an opening month as an exact onset. Keep opening endpoints unresolved until a primary opening/revision publication is obtained.

- Official bureau anniversary https://kotsu.city.kobe.lg.jp/wp-content/uploads/kobekotsu100pamf.pdf states Seishin-Yamate whole Shin-Kobe→Seishin-Chuo opened 1987-03-18, and Hokushin mutual through operation began 1988-04-02.
- Explicit historical outer endpoints in official city plan, search-index excerpt https://www.city.kobe.lg.jp/documents/59179/h28_kobe_city_koukyoshisetsutou-sogokanri-keikaku.pdf states 1988 mutual through operation Seishin-Chuo↔Tanigami. Direct reader returned Internal Error; scope should distinguish indexed excerpt from full PDF review.
- Municipal integration **2020-06-01**: https://kotsu.city.kobe.lg.jp/subway/subway-vehicles/myodani/ explicitly states unified operation began then (its preceding full-line opening date has inconsistent Western/calendar-era text, so use chronology for 1987 opening).

Recommended: validFrom 1988-04-02 for the pattern with chronology source; note current-city IDs represent predecessor Hokushin Kyuko before 2020-06-01. Endpoint status can be supported only with explicitly scoped official city-plan excerpt; directly read chronology alone establishes through onset but not the western terminal of each train.

## Nankai Koya/Semboku

- Direct operator corporate history https://www.nankai.co.jp/company/our_steps/history.html dates mutual Koya/Semboku through operation to **1971-04**.
- Readable operator 140-year history https://www.nankai.co.jp/company/history/chronicle/books/pdf140/nankai_140th_07.pdf , pp.4–6: Nakamozu↔Izumigaoka opening **1971-04-01**, mutual through operation from opening; Toga-Mikita extension **1973-12-07**, Komyoike **1977-08-20**, and Komyoike↔Izumi-Chuo opening **1995-04-01**. These extension openings are physical endpoint constraints; text does not identify Namba as each opening day's through-train terminal.
- https://www.nankai.co.jp/community/station/izumichuo.html directly confirms Izumi-Chuo station opened 1995-04-01.
- Official 2020 Handbook https://www.nankai.co.jp/sites/default/files/imce/pdf/library/company/handbook/pdf2020/03.pdf , p.20 gives through Namba service for Semboku train categories/current Izumi-Chuo line, establishing a 2020 snapshot, not its 1995 opening.
- Same 140-year history p.2 explicitly states absorption on **2025-04-01**; current Nankai line ID is a successor identity.

Recommended: current Izumi-Chuo footprint cannot predate **1995-04-01**; use that as endpoint opening constraint and explicitly keep first Namba-through date unresolved unless new evidence establishes it. Do not set current full footprint to 1971-04-01. Earlier variants may be modeled only at proven shorter endpoints with actual through-terminal evidence; the 1971/1973/1977 physical extension dates alone do not prove every single-seat Namba corridor.
