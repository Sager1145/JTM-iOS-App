# Meitetsu Main / Inuyama regional corridor identity

Research date: 2026-10-13. Scope: Main ordered legs #21 West Biwajima–East Biwajima, #22 East Biwajima–Sakō, #23 Sakō–Meitetsu Nagoya. Read-only primary-source research; no production geometry, IDs, distances, segments, topology, or UI changed.

## Decisive operator evidence

- Meitetsu’s official **Inuyama line** page lists **Meitetsu Nagoya → Sakō → Higashi Biwajima**, then a Nagoya Main connection before Shimo Otai. Its footnote says some stations shown belong to a different line. Thus an Inuyama service label south of its branch does not establish an independent Inuyama physical corridor. [Official Inuyama regional station/service list](https://www.meitetsu.co.jp/train/station_info/line12/index.html).
- The Main-context Sakō station page identifies Sakō and lists Meitetsu Nagoya and Higashi Biwajima as its adjacent Nagoya Main stations. The Inuyama-context Sakō page has the same station suffix `1211` and presents the same station. **Inference:** the two services share Sakō station identity; a basemap Inuyama label cannot by itself justify displacing Main onto another nearby railway. [Main Sakō](https://www.meitetsu.co.jp/train/station_info/line01/station/1211.html), [Inuyama-context Sakō](https://www.meitetsu.co.jp/train/station_info/line12/station/1211.html).
- Higashi Biwajima’s official Main page distinguishes the western Main continuation to **Nishi Biwajima** from the Inuyama continuation to **Shimo Otai**, while its common southern neighbor is Sakō. This supplies the operator’s regional branch identity without identifying yard sidings. [Official Higashi Biwajima station](https://www.meitetsu.co.jp/train/station_info/line01/station/1212.html).
- Meitetsu Nagoya’s official Inuyama-context page explicitly calls its neighbors Sakō and Sannō on the Nagoya Main and gives platform 1 for **Gifu / Tsushima / Inuyama** directions. JR Tokaido/Chuo/Kansai services are separately listed as transfers. This supports the common Meitetsu passenger service approach and excludes a substitution onto JR merely because tracks run nearby. Platform text was used only for service identity, not geometric authority. [Official Nagoya station](https://www.meitetsu.co.jp/train/station_info/line12/station/1201.html).

## Physical corridor decision and remaining limit

The operator evidence supports tracing Main #21 from its western Main branch into the common Meitetsu passenger approach through Higashi Biwajima and Sakō toward Nagoya, preserving separate physical IDs and Main route semantics. **It does not establish an exact georeferenced rail centerline, the exact junction point, individual Sakō platform faces, or whether the proposed 20 m west shift is correct.** Consequently, it supports common station/corridor identity, but does not independently prove the exact physical platform group requested.

The supplied Apple basemap `tsushima-complete-native/meitetsu-main-22-view3-z18-before/basemap.png` was visually inspected: the blue feature labelled Meitetsu Inuyama runs along the long diagonal Sakō station face. That is consistent with the operator evidence. It remains cartographic rendering, not aerial observation or proof of a particular rail/platform centerline. The supplied own-frame normal `[136.87283701223373, 35.18374615617322]` and source physical `005407` `[136.873065, 35.18379]` were not accepted/rejected on this evidence.

[GSI overhead reference centered on Sakō](https://maps.gsi.go.jp/#18/35.183790/136.873065/&base=seamlessphoto&ls=seamlessphoto&disp=1) returned only JavaScript-shell text through the web reader. The corresponding [official photo tile](https://cyberjapandata.gsi.go.jp/xyz/seamlessphoto/18/230739/103671.jpg) was inaccessible through that reader; a local read attempt failed DNS resolution. **No GSI aerial pixels were seen.** Therefore the aerial separation of Meitetsu through tracks from western JR tracks and nearby Meitetsu storage/yard tracks remains an explicit primary-evidence gap. No claim is made that the eastmost black continuation in Main #23, or underground Nagoya physical `005457` `[136.88387, 35.17044]`, has been validated by overhead imagery. No interior station diagram or arbitrary POI was used as geometry authority.

The root reports the page explicitly identified photography from August–September 2020 and displayed a 30 m scale. These observations are supplied by the root; the delegated researcher did not independently view this aerial.

The root-observed pixels show the broad parallel NW–SE railway group, a narrow passenger-station footprint along its eastern side, separate parallel western yard/JR tracks, and Meieki Dori to the east. This supplies **regional separation and spatial context** that the web-reader attempt lacked. In conjunction with the separately cited operator service identity, it supports keeping the common Meitetsu passenger approach distinct from the western tracks. Aerial pixels alone do not identify the railway operator or the use of each western track.

This closes the absence-of-viewed-aerial limitation for the root’s regional context review only. It does **not** validate the exact proposed 20 m Sakō correction, the own-frame rail normal, an individual platform face, the precise branch merge, Main #23’s eastmost black continuation, or Nagoya’s underground physical anchor. All earlier geometric warnings remain applicable; no production mutation or topology decision follows from this note alone.
